Career planning · 5 min

How to choose a career when you feel unsure

Start with curiosity, not pressure. Look for patterns in the subjects and activities you enjoy.

Read article

Write down three activities that make you curious, three tasks you enjoy, and three things you value in a workplace. Explore careers that overlap with those lists. Read real job descriptions, try a small project, and speak with someone who does the work. You do not need to decide your entire future today.

University research · 6 min

How to compare universities fairly

Look beyond rankings and famous names.

Read article

Compare the exact course, accreditation, curriculum, total tuition and living costs, scholarships, location, internships, student support, and entry requirements. Check details on the official university website and note the date you checked them. A university is only a strong option if its course fits your goals and practical constraints.

Study abroad · 7 min

Planning to study internationally

Build a realistic research checklist before applying.

Read article

Research course prerequisites, language tests, application deadlines, visa rules, proof-of-funds requirements, health coverage, and housing. Costs vary substantially by country and city. Use official university and government pages, and discuss major financial decisions with a trusted adult.

Skills · 4 min

Build a small project portfolio

Show what you learned, not just the final result.

Read article

Choose a manageable project connected to an interest: a simple app, a research poster, a short film, a community survey, or a design concept. Explain the question you started with, your process, what changed, and what you learned. Respect privacy and credit sources.

Well-being · 4 min

Make decisions without comparing yourself to everyone

Different people discover their interests on different timelines.

Read article

Use other people's paths as information, not as a deadline for yourself. Break research into small steps, keep notes about what you learn, and ask a teacher, counselor, or trusted adult for perspective when a decision feels overwhelming.

Applications · 5 min

Spot reliable admissions information

Know which pages to trust for requirements and dates.

Read article

Start from the institution's official admissions page. Check the entry year, program name, applicant category, deadlines, required documents, and whether tests or interviews apply. Treat social posts and third-party summaries as leads to verify, not as final authority.
